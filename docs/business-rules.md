# Business rules (PC2)

Rules this mall (**PC2** / `perfect-consign`) enforces. Live Perfect Consign is **PC1**. EMP is archived — [naming.md](naming.md), [emp-to-pc2.md](emp-to-pc2.md).

Add a rule here when it is decided. A rule stays in this list after the code that enforces it changes.

On every form, a required field shows a red asterisk after its label.

## Vendors

A party is one person or one organization, not both. Roles hang off that party: vendor, customer, or wait list. The money key stays the vendor id. The vendor row is the vendor role's books (store take, tax, standing, and booths) and it points at the party. The person or organization row holds names only. Phone, email, and addresses live on the party as child rows.

Staff open everyone at `/{slug}/parties`. That list is identity: person or organization, and which roles they hold. It is not the seller roster. The list shows every party, ten at a time by default (25, 50, or 100 on request), sorted by name, with search across name, kind, and the role words staff see ("consignor" in a consignment-only store); no party is left off however many the store has. The seller roster is `/{slug}/vendors` in a mall and `/{slug}/consignors` in a consignment-only store. A customer page is `/{slug}/customers/{party id}`. A wait-list page is `/{slug}/wait-list/{party id}`. Opening a party goes to that person's seller record if one exists (active or not), else their customer page, else their wait-list page, using only roles that are still active. Removing someone from the wait list or deleting a seller ends that role but keeps the person, so the party page never sends staff to a record that is gone. Owner and manager menus group the seller roster, customers, and parties under People (owners also see Staff there).

A vendor role has to be a person. An organization cannot be a vendor. A vendor can also be given the customer role, so the same person can buy at the register. A customer who is a person can be given the vendor role. An organization customer cannot. The vendor id does not change when a second role is added.

A vendor is Active or Inactive. That is the vendor's own standing. It is not the same fact as which booth they hold. Making a vendor Inactive ends open consignor portal sessions on the next request, burns unused entry links, and bumps credentials on that vendor's password login so open staff sessions end.

A vendor with no booth is still Active. The booth column says Unassigned.

A vendor's name is a first name and a last name. A business name may be empty. When it is present, the list and the receipt show the business name. The payable id does not change.

A party may hold many phones and many emails. One phone and one email are primary. Labels are primary, mobile, home, work, or other. Each value may be empty on create; an email that is present has to be an email address. A party may hold one physical address and one mailing address (street, city, state, ZIP). Mailing may be empty or the same as physical. Screens show a ten-digit US phone as (xxx) xxx-xxxx; other shapes stay as entered.

Store take is a percent of the sale kept by the mall. Settings holds the mall default. On New consignor / New vendor the field is a store take override: leave it blank or match the mall default to follow Settings; a different amount is an override for that seller only and is marked overridden. A saved amount equal to the mall default is the default, and it follows a later change to that default. The sale still posts the commission amount entered on that sale. Agreement dates, tax id, and notes may be empty. An agreement end cannot be before its start.

A vendor is tax exempt only with a resale or tax exempt number on file. That number is required when tax exempt is enabled. When tax exempt is off, the number may be empty.

A vendor cannot be made Inactive while anything is still open between them and the store. The database refuses Inactive, and the page names every reason in one message, for example: "Ada Lane can't be made inactive yet. They owe $100.00 in rent. They still have a booth. End it first." The reasons are rent they owe on any of their stays, money they owe from returns, money the store owes them, a payout balance below zero, a booth deposit the store still holds for them, and a booth they still hold today or later. Each reason clears only by the money moving or the stay ending. Making a vendor Inactive and posting money for that vendor take the same per-vendor lock, so one cannot slip past the other.

A vendor who has left (Inactive, or with no current stay) but still has money open stays in front of staff. The dashboard lists them under Left the store, money still open. Pay vendors lists them under Vendors who have left. The vendor memo list still offers them, marked (has left the store).

A booth is Active or Inactive. That is whether the booth is offered. It is not the same fact as who holds it. The booth list opens on Active. Inactive booths are on the Inactive list. Make inactive is on an empty booth and on that booth's row. Make active is on an inactive booth and on the Inactive list, and it offers the booth again. Making a booth Inactive hides it from that default list, from the assignment choices, and from the map. A current or scheduled assignment refuses Inactive until that assignment ends, and those booths do not show Make inactive. Open rent on a booth whose assignment has ended does not by itself refuse Inactive.

A booth location may be empty. When it is present, it is one line of at most 200 characters, with no leading or trailing space and no line break. The add and edit forms take it, and the booth list shows it. An empty location shows as a blank on that list.

Booth rent can be turned off. While it is off, the mall does not post a rent charge, pay-vendors does not take rent from the check, and the booth, rent, wait list, and map pages stay closed. The Collect rent button on the register is hidden too. A consignment-only store hides the same rent and booth screens. Turning booth rent off is refused while a booth still owes rent.

Consignment and store stock are separate switches. At least one of booth rent, consignment, and store stock stays on. Turning consignment off is refused while a consigned item is still on hand. Turning store stock off is refused while a store item is still on hand. Days on the floor save only while consignment is on. Empty days still mean consigned items do not expire.

An item sits in a booth, on consignment, or in store stock. A booth piece and a consigned piece name a vendor. A store piece does not. A new booth piece needs booth rent on and an open assignment, and it does not expire. A consigned piece needs consignment on. A store piece needs store stock on, has no store-take override, and does not expire. A sale of a store piece credits store sales for the full net and posts no vendor payable. Tax still follows the mall rate. A cost, when present, posts cost of goods and relieves inventory in that same sale.

Buying a consigned piece is one store purchase, and only after it has expired. The expire date must be before today in the mall time zone. A piece that expires today is still on the floor. The item becomes store stock, the expire date clears, and the cost is the amount paid, which may be above or below the tag. A second buy, a buy of a booth piece, a buy of a store piece, a buy of a piece that has not expired, and a buy after the item has sold are refused. A manager may return or donate an expired piece. Only an owner posts the buy.

A markdown schedule is days on the floor and a percent off the original asking price. Percents increase as the days increase. Applying a step writes the prior price and the new price, then updates the asking price. Skipping a step, repeating a step, or applying a step before its day is refused. The schedule is for consigned items.

An expired consigned item that is still on hand cannot be sold until it is returned, donated, or bought. A return sets quantity to zero. A donation makes it store stock with no cost. A booth piece does not reach that gate.

The rent period starts on the cycle day, a whole number from 1 to 28. Day 1 is a calendar month. Any other day runs from that day through the day before it next month. The cycle day cannot change after a rent charge is posted.

Rent belongs to the stay, not to the booth. A stay is one vendor in one booth from a start date to an end date. Every rent charge, rent payment, late fee, booth charge, and booth deposit names its stay, so two vendors who held the same booth never share a balance.

The rent page lists one row per stay per rent period, built by the database, plus empty booths that have an asking rate. Each row shows the vendor, the amount for that period, whether it is charged, and what is still open on that stay. Charge on a row posts that stay's rent for that period. A month that has not started cannot be charged ("That month has not started yet."). The charge is dated today when today is inside the period, otherwise on the later of the period start and the stay start. A charge that was voided can be charged again. A second charge for the same stay and period is refused.

A rent charge for the period a vendor moves in counts the days from the start date through the end of that period, and the amount truncates toward zero. Collect rent names those dates on that charge. A full period shows its charge date. A rent charge for a period the vendor leaves is the full period. Sales can pay that period's rent only up to what the vendor earned. Anything sales do not cover stays receivable and is collected at the register.

A rent payment is split across the vendor's stays by the database. The stay with the oldest unpaid charge is paid first. Inside one stay, rent is paid before late fees. A payment cannot be more than the rent they owe. A credit on one stay pays only charges on that same stay.

## Payouts

A payout no longer clears payable and rent against cash. Close posts a settlement: the smaller of the open payable and the open rent, with no cash. A check, a drawer payment, or a payment from the safe is a later disbursement. A vendor with nothing open gets a report and no journal. Before settlement, close applies open vendor receivable (clawback) against open payable for that vendor — the smaller of the two — with no cash.

A settled or paid run refuses a new journal dated inside its sales window. A reversal is dated after that window. Settlement, rent charged, checks already written, and rent paid in a later window are the net of those journals, reversals included. Print checks does not ask for a check number. The check stock already has the company name and the check number, so the page prints the date, who is paid, the amount, and the memo. Print sales and rent on that page opens the sales and rent sheet for that payout, including a payout for one vendor. That sheet lists the sales on the check, including a sale dated after the closed month, and the rent deducted from them. Two check disbursements cannot share a check number. The Money record Void button refuses settlement and check / cash / safe disbursement journals — undo a paid run with payout reverse, not a generic journal void.

A vendor is pay, credit only, or hold. Credit only and hold are left out of a run. Rent for a credit-only vendor is paid at the desk.

A vendor memo adds to what the mall owes that vendor, or subtracts from it, with a reason. It is dated before the next payout and shows on the statement. A debit may leave the payable below zero. Later sales pay that balance down before a check. While it is below zero the check is zero. The memo does not charge or pay booth rent. A draw, a check, and any other journal still cannot leave the payable below zero.

Vendor receivable (clawback) is what that vendor owes the mall after a return or void when payable was already paid out. It is a control account by vendor. It cannot go below zero. A return after payout debits receivable for the shortfall (and payable only for what is still open). A void after payout first reclasses the shortfall onto receivable, then posts the exact reverse of the sale. The vendor screen shows “They owe us (returns)” when receivable is open.

## Inventory

An item is one item. It has a tag, a barcode, a vendor, a description, an asking price, a quantity, and an intake date. The brand may be empty. The expire date may be empty. Quantity is how many are on hand. A new item defaults to 1. An edit may be 0, and then it cannot be sold.

The tag is unique in the mall, ignoring case. A tag is at most 40 characters. A blank tag is filled with TG, the mall date, and a four-digit sequence. The barcode is unique, ignoring case, and at most 100 characters. A blank barcode is filled with BC, the mall date, and a four-digit sequence. A description is at most 200 characters. The asking price is greater than zero.

Stickers print that barcode. The mall remembers which stickers it uses: Avery 8160 (30-up), Avery 94073 (50-up), a hang tag, two hang tags across, or a jewelry tag. A print can pick another; the sheet picker names the Avery number. Sheet tags show brand, size, and intake date when they fit. A new sheet starts at row 1, column 1. A job stops at 240 stickers. The first sticker preview for an item marks it printed. The items list shows All, Available (quantity on hand), Sold (quantity zero with a posted sale line), Need stickers (never printed), and Qty 0 — each with a count. Each row also shows a Status of On floor, Sold, or Out. Clear drops the search and stock filter. The selected filter is visible, and the page lead names what you are looking at. Column filters and the search box search the whole list. The table shows one page of matches. On Items, check the rows to print; the sheet, how many, and where the sheet starts live on the selection bar with Print stickers. After save or on edit, Print stickers stays on the item form.

The asking price is the price on the item. It is not a journal. A sale still posts the amount entered on the sale. After a sale, that asking price and that consignor stay. A return pays the consignor on the item.

The store take on an item follows the vendor. Saving the vendor's percent, or leaving the field empty, stores no override. A different percent is stored on the item and marked overridden.

The expire date is empty, or on or after the intake date. Settings can set days on the floor, from 1 to 365, while consignment is on. A new consigned item then expires that many days after intake. An empty setting means consigned items do not expire. A booth piece and a store piece do not expire.

After an item is created, the add form stays open with the same vendor and the same intake date. The next item gets a fresh expire date from days on the floor.

The consignor is chosen by custom id or name. The custom id is shown in front of the name. That choice does not create a consignor. Brand, color, category, size, condition, and department are chosen by name on the staff item form, on Instant Add, and on the vendor item form. A name that is not already on the list can be added before the item is saved, including when a similar name is already there. A typed name that was not added is refused. That refusal stays on the form. The Add button stays available for that name and for a different new name. Saving again repeats the refusal until the name is added.

The tag, barcode, brand, description, quantity, store take, intake date, and expire date can be changed. The price and the consignor can be changed until a sale is posted. An item can be deleted only while nothing else points at it. The list shows Delete only then; a held item has no delete step.

A vendor that an item still names cannot be deleted. A brand, color, category, size, condition, or department that anything still points at cannot be deleted. That name can be marked inactive, or merged into another name on the same list. The list opens on active names. Inactive names are on the Inactive list, where Make active puts one back. Inactive leaves the name on the items that already use it and hides it from the next item. Merge moves those items onto the name you keep, makes that name active, and removes the other one. A name that nothing points at can be deleted. Creating the same spelling does not revive an inactive name. An inactive vendor can still own an item. The booth stays on the vendor’s assignment, not on the item.

## Wait list

A wait list row is a person who wants a booth when none are open. It is not a vendor and it is not a journal. The row is keyed by that person's party. Name lives on the person. Primary email and phone live on the party. The wait-list row holds preferred size, status, and notes. Staff still create the vendor and the assignment by hand when a booth opens. Staff open the list at `/{slug}/wait-list` and one person at `/{slug}/wait-list/{party id}`.

First name, last name, primary email, and primary phone are required. An email has to be an email address. Phone is at most 40 characters. Notes may be empty. Notes that are present are at most 2000 characters.

Preferred booth size is a width and a depth in whole feet, or both empty. One side without the other is refused. A size of zero is refused.

Status is Waiting, Contacted, Placed, or Declined. A new row starts at Waiting. The open list is Waiting and Contacted, oldest first. The same email cannot be Waiting or Contacted twice. After the row is Placed or Declined, that email can be added again. Those phone, email, and duplicate-email rules also apply when the party's primary contacts change, not only when the wait-list row is saved.

A row can be edited or deleted. Deleting it does not change a vendor or a booth.

## Customers

A customer is a party with the customer role. There is no customers table. A ticket stores that party's id. Staff add a customer from `/{slug}/customers` (New customer) or by naming someone on a sale. Either path saves the person and the customer role when that name, phone, and email are new; the same contact opens the existing party. Staff open the list at `/{slug}/customers` and one customer at `/{slug}/customers/{party id}`.

A vendor can be given the customer role from the seller page. A customer who is a person can be given the vendor role from the customer page. That is the same party with both roles. The vendor id does not change. An organization cannot take the vendor role, and an organization customer is not edited as a first and last name.

## Registers

A mall starts with one register named Register. The name is unique, ignoring case, and at most 40 characters. The last register cannot be deleted. A register that a shift points at cannot be deleted. Rename is allowed.

One shift can be open on a register. The cashier name is required. The signed-in owner is the default. Opening cash is the amount entered. A bill and coin count replaces that amount. The bills and coins are $100, $50, $20, $10, $5, $1, 25¢, 10¢, 5¢, and 1¢. Zero is allowed. An amount entered with no count is stored as the largest bills and coins that add up to it. The opening count cannot be changed. A closed shift cannot be changed or deleted.

A sale, a cash rent payment, a payout, a stored-value issue, a cash drop, a cash booth deposit, a return of that deposit, a register over/short, and a void of any of those must name the open shift. When more than one register is open and this session has not chosen one, a cash rent payment stays on the rent form and says Choose the active register. A sales-tax payment credits the bank. It does not take cash from the drawer. No open register refuses the post. A sale debits cash, card, checks, a gift certificate, a gift card, or store credit. Each tender is used at most once, at least one is required, and they add up to the sale. A check sale stores the check number. Gift certificates, gift cards, and store credit are redeemed by number, and the open credit cannot go below zero.

Expected drawer cash is the opening count, plus cash sales, cash rent, stored value sold for cash, and cash booth deposits, minus cash reversals, cash payouts, cash drops, and booth deposits returned. A cash drop moves drawer cash to cash in the safe. It is not a payout. The drop date is a mall day from the day the register was opened through today. If that day is not already in an open year, the year is opened for it. A day that was closed stays closed. The Book does not ask anyone to add a period.

Close takes the counted cash you type. A bill and coin count replaces that amount. Card and checks are entered as counted amounts next to the posted totals. Store credit, gift cards, gift certificates, and cash drops are shown and are not counted again. When counted cash matches the expected drawer, close posts no difference. When it does not, close waits until the cashier confirms both amounts, then one journal moves the difference between cash and cash over/short. Card and check differences stay on the shift. Vendor payable and sales tax are never the plug.

Each closed shift has a Z-tape: both counts, the expected-cash lines, the drops, card and checks counted versus posted, and the gift certificate, gift card, and store credit totals.

## Sales

A sale is one or more items on one journal. The customer pays the final sold prices plus sales tax. Store take is the percent of that final price. Tax does not reduce what a vendor is owed.

The sold price starts at the asking price and can differ until pay; every register price change is audited, and a price below that item's floor (when a floor exists) is refused. A discount lowers it. Store take is the item's percent of the final price, otherwise the vendor's, otherwise the mall default, rounded half up to the cent. Sales tax is the mall rate on that final price, rounded the same way. A tax-exempt vendor's items have no tax. A piece marked tax exempt on add or edit item has no tax, including store stock; that mark shows on the register and the receipt.

A discount lowers the sold price. Store take is the item's percent of that final price, otherwise the vendor's, otherwise the mall default, rounded half up to the cent. The vendor is owed the rest of that final price. Percent is half up on each sold price after that line's discount. A flat amount is split across those prices, half up, and the last line takes the remainder. A line discount is that same rule on one line, stored on the line. The ticket discount applies after the line discounts. A discount cannot be more than the price. A zero discount clears it. Tax-free is this ticket only. It stores a reason and every line's tax is zero. Restore tax clears it.

A buyer on the ticket is a vendor. From their sales draws that vendor, still capped at the payable that existed before this sale.

Pay leaves the finished sale on the register: the lines, the tenders, the total, and the change. Scan, Instant Add, and the pad do nothing until New sale. Instant Add follows the inventory name lists: a typed name that was not added is refused, the form stays open, and the Add button stays available until that name is added. Instant Add needs four things, each marked with a red asterisk: the vendor (consignor in a consignment-only store), a description, a price, and a quantity, which starts at 1. The form stops an empty field before it is sent. Every posted form field is trimmed once, when the request is read (`Request::field`; passwords are read as typed), so a field of only spaces arrives blank and the register refuses it with "Choose a vendor." (or consignor), "Enter a description.", "Enter a price.", or "Enter a quantity.", keeps what was typed, and adds nothing to the ticket. The quantity is a whole number of at least 1, and that many go on the ticket. There is no unfinished Instant Add line: every line it adds is complete. The database holds the same rules: an item's description cannot be blank or padded, its price is above zero, a consigned or booth item names its seller, and a ticket line holds at least one. The receipt is that same ticket for a later lookup, a return, or a void. Emailing that receipt posts in the browser as a normal form (not the register fetch swap) so the success or failure flash stays on the receipt.

A layaway is a short payment on the sale tender screen. Cash, card, or a check under the amount due, with a customer on the ticket, debits that tender and credits layaway deposits. The items stay on the ticket and stay unsold. Each deposit and each refund is listed on the layaway receipt, and that receipt prints when the payment is taken. Gift certificates, store credit, and a draw on sales cannot hold a ticket. The sale journal, vendor payable, tax, and store take post only when the deposits and any later tenders cover the amount due. A deposit above the amount due is a sale. Cancel refunds the deposits, prints that refund, and frees the ticket.

Each tender is stored on the open ticket and used once. Cash, card, check, gift certificate, gift card, and store credit are those tenders. Complete posts when they add up to the amount due. The journal debits each tender once. Those tenders add up to the sold prices, minus the discount, plus tax, and the cash-round difference when cash is rounded. From their sales debits that vendor's payable, once, and only up to what the store already owed before this sale. The payable this sale is about to credit does not raise that cap. It credits store take once when that amount is not zero, sales tax once when that total is not zero, and one payable for each vendor who is owed at least one cent. A vendor owed nothing has no payable line. Vendor payable and sales tax are still never the plug.

Pay takes the sold quantity off the item. A void puts back the units that are still sold. A return puts its units back. Zero on hand cannot be sold. The asking price stays on the item.

A gift certificate is sold for cash, card, or a check. That journal debits the tender and credits the certificate. The balance left on a number is its credits minus its redemptions. A charge cannot take more than that remainder.

A ticket is the open cart on one register shift. It is not a journal. The shift has one open ticket. Scanning an item that is already on the open ticket adds one. Open, parked, and layaway tickets together cannot hold more than what is on hand. Asking for more than that (scan or qty) shows: There aren't that many left. The sold price can change until pay. Pay posts the sale and freezes the ticket. Discard removes a ticket only before pay. Close is refused while that shift still has an open ticket.

When the mall rounds cash to a chosen step (cent, nickel, dime, or quarter), that round happens only when the sale is paid entirely in cash. The difference posts to cash rounding. It shows on the register once cash is the only payment, and on the receipt. Card, checks, stored value, and any mix of those with cash stay on the cent. Cash handed over above the rounded cash due is change. It is not a second tender, and the journal debits only the amount due.

Void from the receipt reverses the whole sale and frees every item. A second void is refused. The second-signature check for a cashier or manager void runs in the same database transaction as the reversing journal, so a blank or refused override leaves no void posted. When the payable for that sale was already paid out, void first reclasses the shortfall to vendor receivable, then posts the exact reverse. A return is its own journal for one item. It reverses that item's discount share, store take, and tax, credits the original sale tender by default, and reduces vendor payable only up to what is still open — any shortfall posts to vendor receivable. Crediting a different tender (including cash instead of card) needs a second signature before the return posts. That item can be sold again. The rest of the ticket stays sold. The shopper is not blocked because payable was already cleared.

Suspend Sale (park) takes the open ticket off the shift so close can finish, or so another shopper can be rung. The cashier names the hold in a short modal (optional); a blank name uses the customer or the first item. Suspended Sales lists those holds; Recall puts one back on the active shift. A parked ticket is not drawer cash. Two shifts cannot hold the same item. A parked ticket can be discarded. Its lines cannot be changed until it is recalled.

## Sales tax and reports

The monthly sales-tax worksheet is one calendar month on the mall clock, using posting date. A sale counts in the month it posts. A void counts in the month the reversal posts and carries that ticket's merchandise, discounts, and tax as a negative. A void in a later month stays in the sale month and comes out of the later month. An exempt void reduces exempt sales and does not reduce taxable sales. A return counts on the return's posting date. Exempt sales follow the waiver stored on the ticket, a vendor who is tax exempt, and a piece marked tax exempt.

The filing strip is gross, exempt, returns, taxable sales, and tax collected, then each rate as `8.00% on $100.00`. Eight thousand basis points is 8.00%. The liability strip is what the month started with, what was collected, what was paid to the state, and what is still owed through the end of the month. Still owe is that closing balance. The pay link posts that amount. When it is zero or less, there is no link and the page says nothing is owed. The page shows "Sales tax does not match the books." and no dollar table when collected tax on the taxable, exempt, return, and void rows disagrees with sales-tax payable. Booth rent collected, and gift certificates, gift cards, and store credit issued, are listed as not taxable. Redemptions stay inside the sale they paid. The screen, the print view, and the CSV use that same query. A month that is not a real `YYYY-MM`, including `June`, is an error. It is not rewritten to this month.

Sales are price times quantity, minus both discounts, before tax. A return subtracts the returned amount on the day it posts. The groups are day, vendor, cashier, category, and hour. A missing cashier is "Not recorded". Hour is one day. Tender is a separate column: net money on cash, card, check, gift card, gift certificate, and store credit, tax included, for sales (including reversals) and returns. Tender is not added to the merchandise column. To before From is an error. An empty query is this month.

The rent roll is one row per stay: the booth, the vendor who held that stay, and the open balance, so rent a vendor left owing never moves to the next vendor in that booth. A zero balance stays off the list. The sum must equal booth rent receivable. A receivable line that does not name a stay refuses the page and the nightly job with "The rent roll does not match the books."

Payments are checks, cash, money from the safe, and rent settled against what the vendor owed. A settlement is the vendor-payable debit on that journal. This list is not a balance and is not part of the tie-out.

Gift cards, gift certificates, and store credit are three lines plus a total. Each line must match that account. A gap refuses the page and the nightly job with "Stored value does not match the books."

Expiring items are consigned pieces that expire within 14 days of mall today, including a piece already past that date that is still on the floor. Donated items keep the tag, description, vendor, and quantity from the moment they were given, because donate clears the vendor and a later sale changes the quantity. A donation with no vendor or no quantity says "Not recorded". Sell-through is the sold quantity and merchandise for the range, the quantity on hand today, and a whole percent. The page says on hand is today. When both counts are zero, the percent is blank and that category is left off the list.

Each page and its CSV are the same query. Money on the screen is dollars. Money in the CSV is a decimal a spreadsheet can add. A bad date or group is an error on the page, and the CSV of that same request is that page with no file.

The nightly job refuses when sales tax, the rent roll, or stored value disagrees with the books, and when a role balance disagrees with its journal lines. It also refuses when the books view disagrees with the lines. Sales merchandise is not one account, so the sales report is not part of that tie-out.

A bookmark is a name, one of these sheets, a grouping, and a range preset. Opening it goes to that sheet. There is no free-form filter. The name sales tax is reserved, and a bookmark cannot replace the worksheet.

## Floor, sales, and checks

The day an item sells, the vendor gets a notice from the same net as the vendor check. Subject is Sold today. The body is the vendor name and one line per tag. The Sunday letter starts off. The owner turns Weekly vendor letter on under Building. When it is on, the letter goes to each active vendor whose email has not bounced, including a vendor who owes no rent. Subject is This week. The body is the vendor name, Payable, and Rent you owe. It is filed on Sunday in the mall time zone. Saving money leaves that switch as it is. The rent reminder uses that same body on the rent-cycle day, and only for a vendor who still owes rent. A bounce marks that email address.

Password-reset and receipt emails send in the web request through the mailbox in Settings. Platform admin forgot-password also sends in the web request (platform Gmail/SMTP env). Other store mail (consignor link, owner vendor letter, sale notice, Sunday digest, rent reminder) is filed first and goes out on the mail run (`php bin/work.php`). Platform operator notices for failed Gmail or HTTP 500s stay immediate. A filed letter shows Waiting until the worker marks it Sent (or Bounced). The store home page says when that store has work waiting to run. Another store's queue, and mail that has already gone out, stay off that page. Admin → Mail worker shows whether `STALL_WORKER_PASSWORD` is set, whether the worker heartbeat is missing or older than two minutes, whether site SMTP and platform Gmail are set, the open job queue (store, kind, due time, attempts, subject, recipients, transport, and last error), jobs given up after five attempts, waiting outbox mail, and a pruneable run log. A worker pass emails `STALL_PLATFORM_ADMIN_EMAIL` once when a job is still open more than fifteen minutes past due, and once when a job is given up. A stopped worker cannot send that mail. The host runs `php bin/work.php --forever`.

The owner can file one plain-text letter to every active consignor with a usable email, or to the ones they check. A missing address and a bounced address are left out. An inactive consignor is left off the list.

A mall-wide sale has a start, an end, a percent, and who absorbs it. The percent cannot exceed 50. Two mall sales cannot overlap on dates. The register applies the event that covers today. Mall adds a store discount. Vendor adds a discount that reduces the vendor's price. Booth, check, and mall-sale posts record who posted them.

A floor finding is a missing tag or an item that is not where the system says. A pickup records the person and the day the goods went back to the vendor. A tag that is not on the floor is refused.

A booth charge beyond rent (electric, a sign) is posted on a stay from Booth extras: debit that stay's rent receivable, credit booth extras. The same stay, day, and reference cannot be charged twice ("That charge is already posted today."). Booth deposits are a store setting, off by default. While they are off, Booth extras does not offer them and the database refuses a deposit. A deposit collected at the desk debits cash and credits booth deposits on that stay, and it names the open shift. A deposit release cannot exceed the deposit still held for that stay, and the cash returned names that same open shift. No open register refuses either post. Booth deposits cannot be turned off while the store still holds one. Year-to-date disbursements sit next to the vendor's tax id. The 1099 file waits for an accountant's field list.

The assignment is the lease: the contracted rent is locked when the stay starts and holds until that stay ends. The rent-charge posting key is one stay and one period. A mid-period move-in prorates. The deposit stays on the booth deposits account, on that stay.

The vendor's accepted terms are who may discount, how long goods stay (`keep_days`, a day count from 1 to 3650 when set), and what happens at expiry.

Owner Settings Money has **Require second signature** (off by default). When it is off, a cashier or manager may discount (ticket or line), waive tax, void, change a return tender, waive rent, pay vendors, and correct a booth payable alone; the activity row names that signed-in account. When it is on, a cashier's or manager's discount cannot pass the stored percent ceiling, and a tax-free sale cannot pass the stored tax-free ceiling, unless a second staff member signs as themselves and their ceiling covers it. A cashier or manager void, waive, payout, or booth correction then needs that second signature. The person who signs must be a manager or cashier whose ceiling covers the amount; a books login or vendor cannot second-sign. The activity row still names the cashier who was signed in, and adds the other staff username as the second signer. An owner does not need the second signature either way.

Store activity is `/{slug}/activity` for the owner, a manager, and books. Cashiers and vendors are refused. Each new row names the account that signed in (username, the name stored at the time, and role), the action, and the client address. An operator who entered the store is named by email on that same row. A second signature stays on that row and does not replace the cashier. Rows from before this screen have no account and show as not recorded. Nightly jobs are not a person and do not appear here; their money stays on the journal. Filters are a from day, a to day (a to day before from is pulled up to from), one person, and one action. The person list is the cashiers, managers, books, and owners who can sign in, plus former accounts that still have a row. The action list is the store's own actions. Any other filter is an error. A before cursor and an after cursor together is an error. A cursor that is not a row in that filter says that page is not in this list, and the list stays empty. The page shows 50 rows, newest first, with Older and Newer. Each row leads with the time, the person, the role, and a sentence for the action. The action code, the record, a second signer, an enter-as email, and the address stay under Details. Open is shown only when that role can open the record: a journal, an item that was not deleted, a booth, a vendor or consignor, a customer, an offer, or a paid ticket's receipt. `/{slug}/activity.csv` uses the date, person, and action only. A cursor on that file is refused and no file is sent. More than 5,000 matches is refused with Narrow the dates. A partial file is not sent. The operator page is Operator audit (`platform.admin_audit`): enter-as, wipe, signup, and the other operator actions. It is not this store's activity.

Mall staff roles are cashier (sell, rent, drawer), manager (floor lead: counter plus floor work and read-only money pages), books (read sales, rent, payouts, journals, and reports), and vendor (portal). Email sign-in at `/` opens any of those staff roles and any owner. The owner and manager menus put sellers, customers, and parties under People; the owner Staff link is `/{slug}/staff`. An owner manages Staff with one Add person form (cashier, manager, books, or co-owner) and typed password. Co-owners have the same mall powers as the first owner. Staff can Suspend or Restore a person (blocks sign-in; open sessions end). An owner can change a cashier, manager, or books login among those three roles (not into owner or vendor). An owner may remove another owner; they cannot remove themselves or the last owner on the mall. Suspending must leave at least one active owner. Platform operators are not created on Staff. Vendor portal sign-in stays on the vendor page. A manager cannot open Settings, Company, or Staff, and cannot pay vendors. A manager may return or donate an expired piece and cannot post the expire-desk buy. Owner Settings saves only under `/{slug}/settings/money`, `/{slug}/settings/building`, and `/{slug}/settings/receipt` (plus Backup download); a bare `POST /{slug}/settings` is refused.

A bounced check is a dated journal that debits returned checks and credits checks.

## Late fees

Late fees are a mall setting: on or off, grace days (0–28), a first fee amount, and an amount for each later fee. Turning them on records the effective date as that day. Fees start from that effective date, not from older unpaid history before the feature was enabled.

A late fee posts as its own journal: debit that stay's rent receivable, credit late-fee income. It does not reduce vendor payable. A job posts due late fees through the database, which decides what is due. Each unpaid rent period takes a fee each time grace passes, through the end of that period; the first fee in a period uses the first amount and each later one the next amount. Grace 0 is one fee a day. Fees start over with each new period. A fee is posted at most once: a fee that was waived is not charged again, and the next fee in that period still follows it. A waive is a reversal of that fee journal, with a note of at most 500 characters. Waiving a fee twice is refused. A late fee that tries to hit vendor payable is refused.

## Store use and missed sale

A store use is the mall buying a consigned piece for the business, not for resale. It posts its own journal: expense the agreed price and credit that vendor's payable. Quantity on hand falls. It is not a sale and it does not take drawer cash.

A missed sale pays the vendor their share and expenses that cost to the mall when goods left without a proper sale ticket. It posts through the ledger as `missed_sale`, not as a normal register sale.

## Commission rules

Beyond the item / vendor / mall default store-take path, commission rules can win by specificity, then priority, then price bounds. A posted sale line keeps the commission from the rule that was in force. An equal tie between rules is refused. An offline register snap never trusts a client commission; the post uses the rule engine's take.

## Markdown queue

A scheduled markdown can wait in a queue. The asking price changes when the re-tag is confirmed, not only when the day threshold is crossed. A parked ticket keeps the price it already captured. Changing a floor price needs an audit actor.

## Vendor portal and offline sync

A vendor portal session sees only the vendor named by `stall.vendor_id`. A missing setting, or another vendor's id, returns no rows. A consignor entry link shows a confirm page. The session starts only when that page is submitted. Opening the link does not sign anyone in.

The owner turns **Vendor portal** and **Vendor inventory** on under Building (`/{slug}/settings/building`). The portal starts on. Inventory starts off. Inventory stays off unless the portal is on, and saving the portal off stores inventory off. **Weekly vendor letter** is on that same screen and starts off. Turning it on files the Sunday letter. Portal off refuses vendor password sign-in, an owner preview (`?as=`), and consignor entry (the request and the redeem). The message is “The vendor portal is off.” A correct password while the portal is off is not a failed sign-in. An open vendor session or consignor session ends on the next request. Inactive still ends access on its own.

Portal on with inventory off keeps the check, sales, deals, and the consignor account. The item list, sticker print, add, edit, and delete are not available, and the Inventory link is hidden. The consignor account hides Items and About to expire.

A vendor deal covers all of that vendor’s items, or one item chosen from their own list. The list matches the tag, the barcode, or the description, and each row shows the tag, the description, and the tag price. Pieces still on hand come first. Another vendor’s tag is refused. The vendor pays for that deal. The lookup stays available while inventory is off. The form asks for a bunch at one price, or a lower price each once they buy enough. The sticker price does not change. The check uses the sale price, or the tag price when the store covers the difference.

Your sales opens on Since your last check: the day after the last check through today. A check written today leaves that list empty. The last check is the latest posted check, including one linked on the payout when the line has no journal id. With no check yet, the list starts at the current payout window. This check is that window. Today, Yesterday, This week, This month, and a chosen pair of dates are the other ranges.

Inventory on lets that vendor add one item at a time. The form stays open after a save so they can print that sticker and type the next piece. The store assigns the tag and barcode. The store take stays the vendor’s percent; the form has no store-take field and no cost field. Brand, color, category, size, condition, and department are chosen by name. A name that is not already on the list can be added before the item is saved, including when a similar name is already there. A typed name that was not added is refused. A booth piece needs an open assignment on the mall’s today and does not expire. A consigned piece may expire; a date before intake is refused. When the store does both, the vendor chooses booth or consignment. Store placement is refused.

Edit, while quantity is still on hand, can change the description, price, quantity (including zero), intake, attributes, and a consignment expire date. Quantity zero pulls the piece. It writes no disposition and no journal, and quantity can be put back. After a sale line exists, the price and the vendor stay as stored. When quantity is already zero and a sale line exists, the edit is refused. Delete uses the same in-use check as the desk, so any sale line refuses it. Return, donate, and buy stay at the desk.

The vendor item list colors available rows by whole days from intake through the mall’s today. Today is 0. A future intake is 0. A blank intake is not colored. Quantity zero is not colored. The bands are 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, and 91 or more. The Days on floor column shows that number on a colored row. Column filters and the search box search that vendor’s whole list. The table shows one page of matches. Staff inventory is unchanged. Empty floor-days still means booth pieces do not expire; the colors are age, not expiry.

An offline register snap is minted on the server when the pay form is built for a payable open ticket (lines on the cart, sale not finished): price, line and store discounts, and tax come from that ticket quote (including a tax waiver or vendor tax-exempt), not from browser-edited amounts. Sync posts those frozen figures when the drawer is open and the item still has quantity. Cash posted is net of those discounts plus tax. The register cards’ Unsynced snaps and Refused snaps totals use that same cash figure so the drawer can reconcile offline cash. A tax amount that does not match the minted snap (or the mall rate / waiver / exempt zero) is refused. Duplicate snaps, a closed period, a missing item, a closed drawer, and a second register taking the last unit are refused. `journal_no` stays empty until the post commits. A poster cannot insert free-form snap money, and cannot rewrite or delete a posted snap.

## Sign-in

The site root `/` is the only sign-in page: email and password. The directory maps each sign-in email to one mall and one username. Owner and staff emails share that directory. The same email cannot appear twice across malls or account kinds. A missing or wrong password fails the same way as an unknown email. Every sign-in at either door, store or admin, goes through one gate. The limits are one row in `platform.sign_in_rules`: five failures for one account from one client address (`pair_limit`), forty failures from one address across any accounts (`address_limit`), and twenty failures for one account from any mix of addresses (`email_soft_limit`), all within fifteen minutes (`window_minutes`). An account is the admin email, the store and username, or, for an email that signs in nowhere, that email. A failure before the account's last good sign-in or password reset does not count. `platform.sign_in_begin` takes a lock on the account and the address, counts, and records a refusal in the same statement, so twenty tries at once still count only five. A refused try never checks the password. A wrong password, an unknown email, and a refusal all take the same time and say the same thing. Every try is one row in `platform.sign_in_attempts`: when, which door, the account, the email typed, the address (at most 64 characters), the browser (at most 300), what happened (`ok`, `wrong_password`, `unknown_email`, `locked_pair`, `locked_address`, `locked_email`, `password_reset`), and whether the browser was trusted. A good sign-in remembers the browser: a random cookie whose SHA-256 is one row in `platform.sign_in_devices`. A remembered browser is not refused by the account-wide limit, so a stranger guessing an owner's password cannot lock the shop out; the pair and address limits still apply to it. The first time an account reaches the account-wide limit in a window, the owner (or the admin) and `STALL_PLATFORM_ADMIN_EMAIL` get one email through the platform mailbox; an email that signs in nowhere is refused quietly. A password reset records `password_reset` and ends the count. Each person sees the browsers they signed in from on their Password page and can Remove one; that browser then needs the password like a new one. Admin, Sign-ins lists the newest 200 tries, filtered by email, address, and what happened. Each day the worker runs `platform.sign_in_reap`: tries older than `attempts_kept_days` (90) and browsers not seen for `device_days` (180) are deleted. Platform admin audit records a refused sign-in only when the email matches a known admin.

Bare `/{slug}` and old `/{slug}/login` bookmarks redirect to `/` (or to the signed-in role’s home when the session already belongs to that mall). Forgot password is only `/forgot` (old `/{slug}/forgot` redirects there). It looks up the email in the directory and, when found, sends the reset email in that request through the mall mailbox. The success message is the same whether the email is known or not (anti-enumeration). When the email is known and mail setup or delivery fails, the user still sees that same success flash; operators get a platform notice so the failure is not silent.

An owner or staff email is required for email sign-in. Setting or changing that email is refused when another sign-in already holds it. The account menu Change password screen updates the password and that sign-in email; the mall company email is not a reset fallback. `php bin/set-owner.php` may set `STALL_OWNER_EMAIL` when planting the owner. On the local Docker database, wiping demo stores `mall` or `shop` (CLI wipe, seed reset, or proof cleanup) must leave those owners signable again from `.env` (`STALL_OWNER_PASSWORD` plus `STALL_OWNER_EMAIL` for mall and `desk@cedar-consign.test` / `STALL_SHOP_OWNER_EMAIL` for shop) via `php bin/ensure-demo-logins.php` — empty books are fine until `seed-mall` runs; a wiped store with a dead login is not. When `STALL_ALLOW_TENANT_RESET=1`, the signed-in owner of demo slugs `dev`, `mall`, or `shop` sees Reset Test Data in the header: type `RESET` to wipe that store and reload its sample books (`shop` is the consignment-only demo).

The signed-in mall header shows the date and time on the left, in the mall time zone from Building, and keeps the current second while the page stays open.

Password-reset and consignor magic links use `STALL_PUBLIC_ORIGIN` (not the request `Host`). Mutating POSTs need a session CSRF token. A form that fails that check shows Try again (a fresh copy of that page) and Sign in. When that session already belongs to a store, the same page also links to that store. Secure cookies and HSTS follow trusted TLS only — see `docs/http-edge.md`.

## Platform admin

The signed-in platform header shows the server date and time on the left and keeps the current second while the page stays open.

Platform admin is a private path from `STALL_ADMIN_PATH`, not `/admin` and not an address whitelist. `/admin` is an empty 404 for everyone, including a signed-in operator. The path is one segment of 16 to 63 characters (`a-z`, `0-9`, hyphen) and the segment cannot be `admin`. A missing or illegal path refuses startup and demo deploy. The value stays in the host `.env`. An admin can sign in, turn the authenticator on or off, reset a password, enter a store as a chosen owner (or cashier/manager) in a new browser window (the admin Tenants tab stays open), wipe a tenant, create a store by hand, read Operator audit (operator actions only; a store's own activity is `/{slug}/activity`), open Mail worker for the job queue, a stale-worker warning, and log prune/clear, and open App log to view or clear `var/log/app.log` on this host (clear is audited as `app.log_clear`) and to lift one probe ban (audited as `probe_ban_lift`). A path that contains a dot is an empty 404, except a lowercase store file that ends in one `.csv` or `.json` (activity and report downloads, and the inventory JSON lists). A scanner user-agent is banned on the first request: ZAP, sqlmap, nikto, nuclei, nessus, nmap, masscan, dirbuster, gobuster, ffuf, feroxbuster, wpscan, acunetix, openvas, or zgrab, matched as a whole token. `curl` and `python-requests` are not scanners. The ban limits are one row in `platform.probe_rules`: twenty probe hits from one client address within ten minutes start a ban. A probe hit is `/admin`, a probe-shaped path, or a path no route knows. A missing record, a page the signed-in role cannot open, a 200 or 303, and a missing `/assets/` file do not count. Files that browsers and phones ask for on their own (`/.well-known/…`, `apple-touch-icon` and `favicon` sizes, `site.webmanifest`, `manifest.json`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `browserconfig.xml`) are an empty 404 that never counts. Someone signed in (store staff, owner, platform admin, or a consignor on their own store portal) is never counted and is never refused by a ban. Every ban ends. A ban for too many probe hits always lasts fifteen minutes (`streak_ban_minutes`), so a hostile web page that makes a store's browser send probes can never block that store for longer. A scanner ban climbs: the first lasts fifteen minutes, one that starts again within seven days of the last ban lasts an hour, and the next and every later one a day (`scanner_ban_minutes`). A scanner ban more than seven days after the last ban ended starts over at fifteen minutes. Once a day, when the hour reaches `summary_hour` in `summary_time_zone` (seven in the morning, `America/Los_Angeles`; both in `platform.probe_rules`, with the `summary_enabled` switch), the worker emails `STALL_PLATFORM_ADMIN_EMAIL` through the platform mailbox a list of the bans that started in the day before that hour: address, why, ban number, path, and when it started and ends. Nothing is sent on a day with no bans or when no admin address is set, and the same day is never sent twice. A lifted ban is gone and is not listed. The web role bans only through `platform.probe_hit` and `platform.probe_ban`, which apply those rules. An empty address is an empty 404 and is not stored. The ban uses the request client address only, so an untrusted `X-Forwarded-For` cannot choose who is banned. A banned repeat is an empty 404 with no session and no second log line. Creating a ban writes one line to `var/log/probe.log`. The row stays after the ban ends so the next ban knows how many came before. Lifting it in App log, or with `php bin/lift-probe-ban.php address admin-email` on the server, ends the ban now, forgets the earlier ones, and is audited as `probe_ban_lift`. `php bin/lift-probe-ban.php` with no arguments lists the bans. Product 404s stay in `var/log/app.log` as `page not found`, with method, path, reason, the raw target (query values removed), query keys, address, host, user-agent, accept, and referer. Cookies, the post body, `Authorization`, and query values stay out of that line. Admin sign-in uses the same sign-in gate and limits as the store door. Authenticator is on until a signed-in admin turns it off under Authenticator. While it is on, a correct password does not open the door. The admin then enters a 6-digit authenticator code (30-second step, one step of clock drift) or one unused recovery code. The first sign-in shows a QR code the phone scans, and the same key in groups of four when the camera cannot, then ten recovery codes. The issuer is PC2 and the account name is the admin email. The door stays closed until those codes are confirmed saved. A wrong authenticator code uses the same fifteen-minute lock on its own failure table: five for the email and address, forty from one address, twenty for one email. Turning the authenticator off requires the current password and a current authenticator code, leaves the phone setup in place, and is audited as `admin.mfa_disabled`. A wrong password on that switch counts toward the same admin sign-in lock. Turning it on is audited as `admin.mfa_enabled`. The next sign-in then asks for a code again; if no phone is set up, that sign-in shows the QR code. An admin with no phone set up cannot turn it off. A password reset does not turn the authenticator off and does not remove the phone setup. `php bin/clear-admin-mfa.php email`, run as the database owner, removes the phone setup for that admin and is audited as `admin.mfa_cleared`. The web role cannot clear the secret. Mall owner and staff sign-in stay password-only. The production wipe form still asks for the second admin password only. Password-reset rows, email-verification rows, and admin audit events are append-only. A tenant wipe leaves password-reset and email-verification rows in place, and those rows do not block the drop. When `APP_ENV` is `production` or `prod`, wipe requires typing the store slug, a second different platform admin’s credentials on the same form, and an owner backup recorded for that store within the last seven days. Otherwise, including local development when `APP_ENV` is unset, typing the store slug is the confirmation. The audit `tenant.delete` / `ok` row is written only after `wipe_tenant` succeeds (failure is audited as `failed`). When a second signature is required, wrong second-admin passwords lock only the delete form for fifteen minutes: five failures for that email and client address, or forty failures from that address across emails. Those rows are not admin sign-in failures and there is no email-wide cap, so one signed-in admin cannot lock the other admin out of `/admin`. Unknown email and a wrong password count the same and return the same refusal. A successful second signature clears that email-and-address pair. When `APP_ENV` is `production` or `prod`, error pages stay the generic line even if `APP_DEBUG` is set. A bad admin email is refused. When Gmail (or other outbound mail) fails, the failure is logged with the mail server's reply and a notice is emailed to `STALL_PLATFORM_ADMIN_EMAIL`. Uncaught exceptions and fatal errors that become HTTP 500 also email that address (using the platform Gmail/SMTP env, not the mall’s Gmail row). Store-facing mail uses each mall’s Settings mailbox (Money, Building, Receipt, and Backup under `/{slug}/settings/…`); platform mail (admin reset, signup verify, operator notices) uses the platform Gmail/SMTP env (`STALL_GMAIL_*` or `STALL_SMTP_*`), not the mall Settings row. Admin reset and signup verify send in the web request; operator notices stay immediate. CLI `set-platform-admin` (and demo deploy when `STALL_PLATFORM_ADMIN_PASSWORD` is set) plants that account and leaves `credentials_version` alone when the password already matches; plant CLIs require `STALL_DB_PASSWORD` and refuse invalid DSN pieces.

## Self-serve store signup

Public Create your store at `/signup` is off until a platform admin turns it on under Admin → Signups. The owner always confirms the email before anything is built; there is no switch to skip that. The form asks for the store name, email, and password. A rejected form keeps those fields filled and shows the error in red. The browser solves a small check before Create store turns on: `platform.signup_challenge` gives a one-time seed for that client address, and the page finds a number whose SHA-256 of `seed:number` starts with `proof_bits` zero bits. Without JavaScript the page says to turn it on. Every rule is one row in `platform.signup_rules`: a link works for `pending_hours` (24), one client address may ask `per_address_per_hour` times (5), one email `per_email_per_hour` times (3), all addresses together `pending_cap_per_hour` times (200), the check uses `proof_bits` (18) and a seed lasts `proof_minutes` (30), and finished signups are kept `kept_days` (30). The web role asks only through `platform.signup_request`, which applies all of those rules in one statement. A new email gets one pending row in `platform.store_signups` (the password hash and the SHA-256 of the link, never the link) and a mail with the link. An email that already signs in gets a "You already have a store" mail with the sign-in and forgot-password links, and nothing is stored. A new request for an email that is still waiting replaces the old link. An email asked too often, or one whose store is being built, gets no mail. The visitor sees the same sentence in all of these cases, so the form never tells whether an email is on file. A spent or unknown check is "Your browser did not finish its check. Try again." Too many from one address is "Too many signup attempts. Try again later." The platform cap is "Signups are busy right now. Try again in an hour." Opening the link shows a Set up my store button and changes nothing. Pressing it confirms the email and files one `store-provision` job; pressing it again changes nothing. The link page then waits and checks again every five seconds. The worker takes the signup into building and switches to `stall_provisioner`, a role that cannot sign in and is not a superuser, for one transaction that creates the store, its poster role, its grants, and the owner (email confirmed), marks the signup ready, and clears the password hash. A store's platform rights come from `platform.grant_poster_platform`, which the installer owns, and `platform.assert_poster_grants` refuses the build if one is missing. A taken address gets `-2`, `-3`, and so on; the reserved slugs are skipped. The worker then files one "Your store is ready" mail through the platform mailbox, with a sign-in link that fills in the email. When it is ready the link page sends the owner to sign in. A build that fails rolls back completely, is marked failed with the reason, and the job tries again; the link page says it is taking longer and that an email will follow. An expired, replaced, or discarded link is "That link is invalid or expired. Start again at Create your store." The web role cannot read a password hash or a link hash, cannot write a signup row, and cannot become `stall_provisioner`. Each day the worker runs `platform.signup_reap`: unconfirmed signups past their deadline are deleted and only counted in `platform.signup_reaped`; ready and discarded rows go after `kept_days`; failed rows wait for an operator. Admin → Signups lists the waiting, confirmed, building, failed, and ready signups with Resend link (a waiting signup gets a new link and the old one stops working) and Discard (waiting, confirmed, or failed), shows how many unconfirmed signups were removed in the last 30 days, and lists stores built before this rule whose owner never confirmed the email, each with Delete store (the usual wipe). Admin → Tenants → Create store goes through the same queue with the owner email already confirmed and works when public signup is off. Audit rows are `store.signup_toggle`, `store.email_verified`, `store.ready` (written by the worker), `store.admin_create`, `store.signup_resend`, and `store.signup_discard`. CLI `set-owner` marks the planted owner verified. Sign-in is email + password; signup never talks about a separate sign-in name.

## Loyalty

Loyalty is not scheduled. Points, an earn rate, and a redemption journal stay unbuilt until the tenant says whether the mall, the vendor, or a split absorbs a redemption. Customers can already be named on a ticket.
